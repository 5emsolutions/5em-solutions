What we run into on Rocky Mount houses

Rocky Mount has two very different kinds of deck problems. Out in Englewood, Westridge and the neighborhoods off Sunset, you have brick ranches with decks that were added on in the eighties and nineties — often by a previous owner, often without a permit, and almost always with the ledger board lag-bolted straight through the siding into who knows what. That is the single most common reason a deck comes off a house in this state. We pull the siding, find the band board, bolt to it properly, and flash it so water runs out instead of in.

Then there is the older housing near Villa Place and along Falls Road: 1920s and 1930s wood-frame homes with deep front porches, real tongue-and-groove porch floors and turned columns. Those are a different job. You cannot rip a porch like that off and bolt a pressure-treated box to the front of the house — it would look wrong and it would hurt the value of the house. We rebuild them in kind, matching the profile of the original trim.

Rocky Mount also gets weather. We are far enough inland that we do not get the storm surge, but we get the wind and we get the rain sitting on things for three days. A deck built here needs blocking, needs real fasteners, and needs the framing detailed so water leaves it.

Rocky Mount questions

Do I need a permit for a deck in Rocky Mount?

Usually yes, and which office depends on which side of the line you are on — Nash County, Edgecombe County, or the City of Rocky Mount. We handle the permit and schedule the inspection. It is included in the price we quote you, not a surprise afterward.

Can you match an old porch instead of replacing it with something modern?

Yes. On the older houses off Falls Road that is usually the right call. We match the column profile, the rail spacing and the floor board width so it reads as the same porch, just one that is not rotting.
