What we run into on Greenville houses

Greenville housing splits about three ways, and each third breaks differently. The brick ranches off Elm Street and Fifth were built in the fifties and sixties with wood fascia, wood soffit and shallow overhangs — that trim is now sixty years into an Eastern NC climate and most of it has gone at least once. The rentals around ECU take a different kind of beating: drywall damage, doors, floors, and interiors that need turning over between tenants on a schedule that does not move. And the subdivisions out toward Brook Valley and south along Fire Tower are new enough that the framing is fine and old enough that the builder-grade decks are failing.

We do all three. Landlords call us because we show up on the turnover date and finish inside it. Homeowners call us because we will tell them the difference between a roof that needs replacing and a pipe boot that needs forty dollars of attention.

One Greenville-specific thing worth knowing: a lot of the older neighborhoods sit low enough that crawlspaces stay damp most of the year. That shows up as soft subfloor near exterior walls and in bathrooms long before it shows up anywhere you would look. If a floor feels springy, that is worth a call before it becomes a joist problem.

Most common calls in Greenville

  • Soffit and fascia rot on fifties and sixties brick ranches with shallow overhangs
  • Turnover work on student and single-family rentals — drywall, paint, floors, doors
  • Twenty-year-old subdivision decks with ledgers bolted through the siding
  • Soft subfloor from damp crawlspaces, especially near exterior walls and baths
  • Storm and wind damage after the systems that come up the Tar River valley
