What we run into on Wilson houses

Wilson has some of the best housing stock in this part of the state and some of the most expensive-to-ignore. The historic district off West Nash Street is full of two-story frame houses built with tobacco money — twelve-foot ceilings, deep porches, real wood siding, and original trim that has been painted twenty times. When that trim finally goes, it goes at the bottom of the columns and behind the gutters, where nobody looks.

Those houses are worth doing right. Replacing an original porch column with a hollow fiberglass one changes the whole face of the house. We would rather build one out of cellular PVC or clear cedar to match the profile you have. It costs more the day we do it and it costs less over the twenty years afterward.

The other half of our Wilson work is ordinary and just as important: reroofs on 1960s ranch houses, drywall repair after a leak, kitchens, and exteriors that have not been painted in fifteen years and are now three years past the point where paint alone would have fixed it.

Wilson questions

Do you work on older homes in the historic district?

Yes, and we work on them the way they ought to be worked on. We match profiles, we do not cover original detail with vinyl, and we will tell you when something is worth saving instead of replacing.

My house has not been painted in fifteen years. Is paint enough?

Maybe not, and we will tell you before you spend money. If the wood is soft, paint is a coat of hope over a rotting board. We will walk it with you, probe the questionable spots, and give you one number that covers fixing it and painting it.
